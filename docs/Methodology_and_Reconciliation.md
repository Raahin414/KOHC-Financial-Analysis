# Methodology and reconciliation guide

The submission contains exactly **27 distinct measures** for five companies over FY2021–FY2025. These include all 19 university requirements, the four further Table 4.2 measures—debt-to-capital, ROIC, BEP and EV/EBITDA—and four selected PPT additions: NWC, receivables turnover, DIO and BVPS. Overlaps are counted once.

The other seven former headline measures were removed from the company tables, sample averages and charts. EBITDA remains a supporting calculation for EV/EBITDA. Their previous verification and the complete 63-case investigation remain in the audit as historical evidence.

The primary reference is the supplied Table 4.2 from Brigham & Houston’s *Fundamentals of Financial Management*, Chapter 4. The university’s *Project Guidelines*, PDF pp 5–6, establish the 19 mandatory measures and the n/m and fiscal-year-end price rules. The supplied TA clarification permits the balance choices explained below.

The class deck **Financial Management_Chapter 04 - IMP RATIO ANALYSIS.pptx** supports NWC on slide 2, receivables turnover and DIO on slide 3, and BVPS on slide 7. Slides 3–6 also provide useful average-balance formulas. Slide 8 defines EBITDA as EBIT plus depreciation and amortization. ## A. Closing and average balances

| Receivables Turnover and DSO | Average opening/closing net trade receivables | LUCK and MLCF support averages over multiple years. KOHC and DGKC use closing receivables. | Fixed Asset Turnover | Closing net PPE, including CWIP | MLCF and generally LUCK use closing net PPE; KOHC uses closing operating PPE. DGKC uses average PPE. One closing net-PPE definition avoids mixing operating-only and total-PPE denominators. |
| Total Asset Turnover | Closing total assets | Closing assets reproduce KOHC, LUCK and MLCF. FCCL’s DuPont presentation also supports this basis, although its labelled turnover table is inconsistent. DGKC’s explicitly average-based result is kept separately. |
| ROA, ROE and BEP | Closing assets or common equity | Closing equity reproduces KOHC, MLCF and FCCL ROE. LUCK uses comprehensive income/closing equity; DGKC reports averages. | ROIC | Closing interest-bearing principal plus common equity | No identical issuer ROIC series was identified. Closing capital is the stated project choice, consistent with the common closing return basis. Liquidity, debt and share-value balance ratios use closing balances. EPS uses the appropriate audited weighted ordinary-share denominator on the compatible market-price basis. BVPS uses closing shares excluding treasury shares. No opening balance was replaced with a closing balance: audited FY2020 openings remain available for the averages and growth analysis.

The audit contains 422 closing/average tests. A numerical match suggests a possible convention; it does not prove formal adoption. Every main sample mean uses the same formula and balance basis for all five companies. Differences in the issuer reconstructions do not enter those means.

## B. Financial-statement classifications

**Inventory and fixed assets.** Inventory includes stores, spares and stock-in-trade for all companies. MLCF’s published series excludes stores. Its freight reclassification also explains older margins and turnover: FY2021 cost 28,135.419 less freight 543.688 equals 27,591.731; FY2022 cost 36,244.156 less freight 699.664 equals 35,544.492. Distribution expenses increase by the same amounts, leaving operating profit and PAT unchanged. The later historical summary is reconstructed separately; original audited earlier-year inputs are not silently overwritten. Sources: MLCF2022 PDF p 239 and 2025 PDF pp 169,175. Net PPE includes CWIP in the final turnover comparison; the shorter operating-PPE denominator remains a separate company convention.

**Debt definitions.** Debt ratio means total liabilities/assets. Debt-to-equity means interest-bearing principal and leases/common equity, using the same financing scope as debt-to-capital. The guidelines name D/E without defining its debt numerator; the interest-bearing interpretation is the selected textbook-aligned definition. KOHC's published 4:96 D:E presentation is a rounded capital mix, not a 4% debt/equity quotient; its debt share and the textbook D/E require different denominators. Operating payables, tax liabilities, grants and accrued interest are not interest-bearing principal. Interest-free parent financing is also excluded from that principal definition. These choices apply to every company.

**Cash and equivalents.** Cash ratio now includes the cash-equivalent investments specifically identified in audited cash-flow component notes, and excludes disclosed pledged balances. Borrowing netting is reversed so that cash remains a gross asset while borrowings remain in liabilities. This avoids counting borrowing twice in EV. LUCK’s qualifying funds and lien adjustments, MLCF’s FY2024 cash-equivalent investment, DGKC’s FY2025 mutual funds and FCCL’s pledged balances are traced in **EV Inputs**. Other short-term investments are not automatically treated as cash. Relevant notes: LUCK2022 p 259 and 2025 p 275; MLCF2025 p 268; DGKC2025 p 212; FCCL2025 p 285.

## C. EBIT, interest, taxation and EV/EBITDA

The project’s textbook EBIT is **profit before tax and levy + expensed interest**. The same definition drives operating margin, TIE, BEP, NOPAT and EBITDA. Interest includes expensed bank, lease and other identified financing interest; cash interest paid is not substituted for the accrual expense. Bank charges and non-interest exchange differences remain expenses. Adding back all finance costs instead would give a different numerator; those issuer diagnostics remain in the audit.

Core operating profit before other income remains a separate income-statement line. EBIT can exceed gross profit when other recognized income is substantial. For example, KOHC’s investment income contributes to EBIT. An EBIT margin therefore should not be described as the cement segment’s pure production margin.

For ROIC, **T = (audited levy + income-tax expense) / profit before tax and levy**. NOPAT is EBIT×(1−T), and invested capital is interest-bearing principal plus common equity. The actual tax charge includes deferred and other audited tax effects. There is no arbitrary statutory override or tax-rate cap. DGKC FY2023 has negative NOPAT and displays n/m; its signed calculation remains in the audit.

**EV = official year-end price × closing shares + book interest-bearing principal + other financing claims − available cash equivalents.** Book financing is an explicit approximation because reliable traded debt values are unavailable. Other claims include identified interest-free financing and accrued financing interest, including embedded amounts. Ordinary equity is used; the selected standalone/unconsolidated statements do not introduce a non-controlling-interest claim. No hypothetical preference or other claim is invented.

EBITDA is EBIT plus audited PPE/ROU depreciation and intangible amortization, once each. The D&A review corrected LUCK FY2021: depreciation 4,298.760 plus amortization 10.653 equals 4,309.413 million, rather than the former 4,298.760 total (LUCK2022 PDF p 258). LUCK FY2020 deferred tax was also corrected from 5,167.161 to 5,167.561 million using note 24.2, which reconciles the printed liability subtotal (2021 PDF pp 191,216). These corrections and the prior values remain visible in the audit.

## D. Market prices and share bases

Official PSX fiscal-year-end closes are unchanged:30 June 2021,30 June 2022,27 June 2023,28 June 2024 and 30 June 2025. The earlier session is used only where year-end was not a trading day. Annual means, medians and candidate dates are diagnostic evidence only.

KOHC’s split trading began 25 August 2025, after the FY2025 measurement date, so the FY2025 project price remains 379.36 on its pre-split basis. LUCK’s FY2025 price and per-share figures use its verified post-split basis. Other verified bonus/share adjustments are preserved. Historical split-restated company EPS is compared on its reported basis in the audit, while the submission keeps the price and shares compatible at each FY-end.

Published per-share conventions explain several differences. LUCK FY2023’s historical EPS corresponds to opening issued shares rather than the audited weighted count. MLCF FY2024’s published BVPS uses weighted shares; the submission uses closing shares. These are numerical reconstructions, not proof of a uniform issuer policy across all years.

## E. Published information that remains unconfirmed

The final audit retains 455 corresponding official published observations.437 can be numerically reproduced at reported precision, including six KOHC source-inconsistency diagnostics. This does not mean 437 company formulas were expressly disclosed or that normalized project values must match them.

The remaining 18 consist of eight FCCL asset-turnover observations contradicted by its own sales/assets and DuPont presentation, and ten cases without a conclusive cause: DGKC FY2021 cash ratio; FCCL FY2025 TIE; FCCL P/E for all five years; and FCCL DSO for FY2023–FY2025. The audit shows the tested inputs and failed alternatives. No price, income, debt or receivable figure is changed to manufacture agreement.

Two further official presentation problems remain important even where a numerical reconstruction is possible. KOHC’s latest market-to-book history applies a selective extra fivefold reduction to a tangible-asset diagnostic, while the project correctly uses equity BVPS. KOHC’s FY2024 comparative EPS also uses internally inconsistent weighted-share counts. LUCK ROE is reproduced by total comprehensive income/closing equity rather than net income/common equity. These explanations are grouped in the audit, with individual company-year evidence retained.

## Defending the approach in a viva

- Start with the ratio’s purpose and its numerator and denominator. Distinguish all liabilities from interest-bearing financing, and core operating profit from the defined EBIT.
- Explain that a sample average is meaningful only when companies use the same definitions. An issuer’s published convention is reconstructed separately rather than mixed into the sample.
- State the balance basis shown beside the formula. Closing choices reflect the reviewed practices and common normalization; averages are used where stated, with verified openings.
- For a mismatch, identify the actual cause supported by evidence—timing, inventory scope, freight classification, interest inclusion or share basis. If the cause is unknown, say so and show the official inputs used.
- Describe EV/EBITDA as a book-financing approximation using actual PSX equity prices, not a precise traded enterprise valuation. Do not infer that a lower multiple alone proves undervaluation.

Negative or non-meaningful project observations display **n/m** and are excluded from sample means and charts. Zero dividends remain valid zero observations. Signed source figures and raw calculations are retained. Vertical analysis preserves accounting expense signs; horizontal analysis follows the existing guideline convention for negative or undefined growth.
