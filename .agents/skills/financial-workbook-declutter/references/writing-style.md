# Concise financial explanations

Keep calculation definitions and accounting decisions distinct from interpretations.

Use “We average opening and closing inventory because sales and costs cover a full year.” Avoid “Temporal balance normalization supports comparability.” Explain a source difference once in the existing methodology area rather than repeating it for every year.

For ratio movements, separate the observed value, its numerator/denominator movement, the officially supported business cause and the implication. If only the arithmetic is established, say that. An inventory-days increase does not by itself prove weak demand.

Preserve qualifiers when shortening. “Published P/E uses another price” overstates the evidence if that convention is unknown. Prefer “Our P/E uses the official year-end price; the company's exact published price convention could not be confirmed.”

Do not make `n/m` look like missing data. A signed working-capital deficit remains a real result even if assignment guidelines require its displayed ratio to be `n/m`.

Dividend yield uses dividends attributable to the financial year under the project's documented convention. A cash-flow payment can settle a previous declaration. Explain this once where a short display annotation could otherwise mislead.

Retain source URLs and accounting definitions. Remove repeated assurances, decorative status badges, generic caveats and internal authoring language. Do not claim figures are error-free because presentation checks passed.
